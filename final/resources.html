3 Waypoint three: Connect

The search moves faster with company

Applications open doors, but most roles still get filled through a warm introduction. Here's how to build and use your network without it feeling transactional.

Networking Basics

Four habits that make networking feel natural

Ask for information, not a job

Request a 15-minute informational interview to learn about someone's role or company. It's a lower-pressure ask, and referrals often follow naturally.

Start with your alumni network

Shared schools are an easy, low-friction reason to reach out. Most alumni are glad to spend 15 minutes helping someone from their program.

Warm intros beat cold applications

A short note asking a connection to introduce you to someone on a team you're targeting dramatically improves your odds of a reply.

Follow up on a schedule

A polite check-in two weeks after a conversation, and again a month later, keeps you on someone's radar without feeling pushy.

Professional Development

Keep building while you search

Earn a focused certification

A short, well-regarded certificate in your field signals momentum to employers and gives you something new to talk about in interviews.

Join a professional community

Slack groups, local meetups, and industry associations surface unlisted openings long before they hit a job board.

Subscribe to one industry newsletter

Staying current on your field gives you sharper, more specific answers to "why this industry" in interviews.

Trail Markers

Outside resources worth bookmarking

    LinkedIn

    Keep your profile aligned with your resume and use it to research companies before you apply. linkedin.com

  • 02

    Glassdoor

    Read company reviews and salary ranges before an interview, so you walk in with realistic expectations. glassdoor.com

  • 03

    The Muse — Career Advice

    Practical, well-written articles on resumes, interviews, and workplace questions. themuse.com/advice

  • 04

    Your local library's career center

    Many public libraries offer free resume reviews, mock interviews, and job-search workshops — worth checking before paying for a service.
