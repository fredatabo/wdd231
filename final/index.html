1 Waypoint one: Prep

Get trail-ready before you set out

A tight resume and a rehearsed interview voice are the gear you carry into every application. Sort those out here, then move on to Waypoint 2 to start the search.

Resume Essentials

Six things every resume needs to get right

  • Tailor it to the role. Mirror the language of the job posting — recruiters and applicant-tracking systems both scan for it.
  • Lead with impact, not duties. "Cut onboarding time 30% by rebuilding the training doc" beats "Responsible for onboarding."
  • Keep it to one page early on. Two pages is fine past ~8 years of experience; before that, cut ruthlessly.
  • Keep formatting boring and consistent. Same date format, same bullet style, same font throughout — save the personality for your portfolio.
  • Cut the objective statement. Use that space for a two-line summary of what you actually bring, or skip it entirely.
  • Proofread with fresh eyes. Read it out loud, or have someone else read it — typos are the fastest way to lose a callback.
Interview Compass

Six questions you should already know how to answer

"Tell me about yourself."

Give a 60-second arc: where you are now professionally, one or two relevant highlights, and why this role is the logical next step. Not your life story.

"What's your greatest weakness?"

Name a real, specific weakness and the concrete habit you built to manage it. Skip the humble-brag disguised as a flaw.

"Why do you want to work here?"

Reference something specific about the company's product, mission, or recent work — proof you researched them, not a generic answer that fits any employer.

"Tell me about a time you faced a challenge."

Use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Spend most of your time on the Action and end with a measurable Result.

"Where do you see yourself in five years?"

Talk about the kind of skills and responsibility you want to grow into, tied loosely to this role's natural path — not a job title guess.

"Do you have any questions for us?"

Always say yes. Ask about team structure, what success looks like in 90 days, or a recent challenge the team faced — never "just" salary and vacation on a first round.

Field Notes

General career-readiness advice

Match your LinkedIn to your resume

Recruiters check both. Inconsistent dates or titles read as sloppy at best, dishonest at worst.

Build a lightweight portfolio

Even a single page with three examples of your work gives interviewers something concrete to ask about.

Practice out loud

Answers that sound great in your head often ramble out loud. Rehearse with a timer or a friend before the real thing.

Send a thank-you note within 24 hours

Short and specific beats long and generic — reference something you actually discussed in the interview.

Track every application

A simple spreadsheet with company, role, date applied, and status saves you from following up blind or double-applying.

Set a weekly application rhythm

A steady handful of well-tailored applications each week outperforms a sporadic flood of generic ones.
